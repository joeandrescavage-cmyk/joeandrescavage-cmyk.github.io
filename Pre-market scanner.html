Before every buy

New here? How to use this page

A simple daily routine (Eastern time)

  1. After 8 PM: run Tomorrow’s watchlist. It finds after-hours movers and news since the close. Pick 2–3 names to study.
  2. 8:15 AM: run the Morning scan. It shows who is actually gapping with real volume. Don’t buy pre-market.
  3. 9:30 to 9:46: let the opening settle. Prices swing wildly in the first minutes.
  4. 9:46 onward: run Right now. Look for stocks near their highs, above their average price, with steady volume.
  5. Every buy: open the stock’s Plan, enter the order, and set the stop and target immediately.
  6. 3:30 PM: sell what isn’t working. Log your account value after the close.

How to read the bars

  • Checklist counts how many of the page’s rules a stock meets (real news, no red flags, volume, trend, history). It is not a buy signal. A high bar means fewer reasons to skip it.
  • Odds up is a base rate from history: how often similar gaps finished above their open. Below 50% is normal, since most gaps fade.
  • The small +15% and −7% numbers show how often those levels were touched at some point. A high −7% number means tight stops get hit.

Rules that protect you

  • Decide your maximum loss before you buy. Use the Position sizer.
  • Stops don’t work before 9:30 or after 4:00, and gaps can jump past them.
  • Never add to a loser. Never chase after a spike.
  • Under $1, spreads are wide and exits are hard. Keep those tiny.
  • If you hit your daily loss limit, you’re done for the day.

Words you’ll see

  • Gap: how far the price opened above yesterday’s close.
  • VWAP: the day’s volume-weighted average price. Above it means buyers are in control.
  • Pre-market / after hours: thin extended-hours trading. Limit orders only.
  • Red flag: an offering, reverse split, listing warning, or going-concern note. These often mean selling pressure.

This page is a research tool, not advice. Most small stocks that spike fade, so size every trade as money you can lose.

Pre-market volume and price come from consolidated market data delayed 15 minutes (the free plan’s limit), refreshed with the latest IEX trade where one exists. A high score means unusual pre-market interest, not a prediction. Stocks that gap up often fade after the open, especially when a stock offering is announced. Treat this as a shortlist to research. Odds up is a historical base rate: the share of past stocks with a similar gap (or, in the evening list, a similar up day) that finished above their open, from the last ~6 months of low-priced stocks and nudged by that stock’s own record. The small line shows how often those cases touched +15% and −7% at some point. It isn’t a prediction. In Right now it uses the stock’s gap at today’s open and shows where the price is versus that open.
